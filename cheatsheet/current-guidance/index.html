2024 Handbook Cross-check

Cross-checked against FMCSA's Medical Examiner's Handbook 2024 Edition (120 pp) and the 2024 Handbook Update Summary; the certification-period table below is rebuilt from those public sources and the regulations. The items in this section are where the current guidance differs in emphasis from the older tables still taught in most course materials — know both.

The big one: fixed waiting periods and 1-year intervals are OLD guidance

Older guidance (Appendix A tables, 2002 Cardiovascular Advisory Panel) — still taught in most training courses 2024 ME Handbook (current)
Waiting periods (MI 2 mo, CABG 3 mo, PCI 3 wk, AF 1 mo, TBI 3 yr, stroke 1/5 yr, depression 6 mo/1 yr, etc.) Given as specific numbers Removed. The 2024 Handbook drops the prescriptive guidance — no waiting periods and no certification periods beyond what the regulations state; the ME makes an individual assessment using listed considerations
Cert interval for cardiac conditions (MI, CABG, PCI, HF, AF, SVT, VT, pacemaker, aneurysm, PE, stable angina, transplant, congenital HD) 1 year "Up to 2 years" — only the regulation's 2-yr max applies
Thresholds (>6 METs, LVEF ≥40%, FEV1 <65%, PaO2 <65, 120° perimetry, neck-size cutoffs (see OSA note below), Mallampati) Given Mostly not stated in the 2024 MEH (LVEF bands 55–70/40–54/35–39/<35 and antihistamine "12 hours" are still in the MEH)
Numbers that ARE still fixed (regulation or 2024 MEH) — HTN stages (1 yr / 3 mo / 6 mo); epilepsy 10 yr; single unprovoked seizure 5 yr; LOC not-seizure 6 mo (recommended); ITDM 12 mo & 3-month records; AVS 1 yr; 45-day forms; exemption criteria (8 yr / 4 yr)

How to use this on the exam. The exam is based on the regulations, which have not changed (FR 2024-01056: the Handbook amends no regulation). Regulation-based numbers (above, right column) are safe to answer literally. For discretionary conditions, if an answer choice says "evaluate on a case-by-case basis / individual assessment / consult the treating clinician," that is the 2024-handbook answer; if the scenario reads like a classic practice-question pattern (e.g., "post-MI 3 months, satisfactory ETT, EF 48%, cleared → 1 year"), the expected answer follows the older table. When both appear, the case-by-case option is usually the "most correct."

Other 2024-handbook confirmations

  • Methadone (Schedule II) "does not automatically preclude certification" — ME needs prescriber's statement; final call is the ME's. (Older training materials listing methadone as an automatic disqualifier are superseded.)
  • Coumadin references removed; anticoagulation judged by the underlying condition. No INR-frequency rule in the 2024 MEH.
  • Disabled ICD: MEH adds recommendations for certification with a disabled ICD (case-by-case); active ICD still fails the CV standard.
  • Hand loss = all five fingers; fewer fingers or any toes → (b)(2). SPE only for limb, never spine/torso. MER must record location/extent, how/when, prosthesis type.
  • Hearing exemption application details added; AVS: not certifiable if deficiency unstable or insufficient adjustment time.
  • OSA: no regulatory screening, testing, compliance, waiting period, or interval requirement; multiple risk factors → consider sleep study; retesting per treating provider. (The 2016 MRB recommendations' actual figures: neck > 17 in male / > 15.5 in female; BMI ≥ 40, or 33–40 plus ≥3 risk factors — the "neck 17/16 + BMI > 35" version is a common classroom simplification; neither set is in the MEH.)
  • Neuromuscular: "severe functional impairment does not physically qualify"; MS and Parkinson's case-by-case; MEH gives no specific recommendations for congenital myopathies, metabolic muscle disease, motor neuron disease, myasthenia, peripheral neuropathy.
  • Stroke: recurrence 10–15%, mostly within 1 yr; TIA → 1 in 3 eventually stroke; cortical/subcortical lesions raise seizure risk, cerebellar/brainstem do not; a single post-stroke seizure may constitute epilepsy.
  • ESRD / non-insulin diabetes / other uncovered conditions → evaluate under the most applicable standard.
  • Privacy: MER never to employer without driver consent, even when employer pays; driver always entitled to a copy.
  • HTN Stage 2 range is 160/100–179/109 (some course materials misprint the upper diastolic bound).
