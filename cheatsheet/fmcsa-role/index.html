FMCSA and the Medical Examiner's Role

FMCSA / NRCME structure

  • FMCSA = 1 of 9 DOT administrations; created by Motor Carrier Safety Improvement Act of 1999; HQ Washington, DC.
  • FMCSA's Medical Programs Division administers the medical program: oversees the National Registry (NRCME) and the national certification process, the medical regulations, and the Medical Review Board; runs the exemption and certificate programs.
  • FMCSA Large Truck and Bus Crash Facts (2017): 13% of fatal crashes involved a large truck/bus; ~4 of 5 deaths were occupants of other vehicles [FMCSA data]. 1999 Mother's Day crash (22 dead): NTSB — driver incapacitation; driver had been certified despite disqualifying conditions.

Regulations vs. guidance

Regulations Guidance (e.g., Medical Advisory Criteria, Appendix A to Part 391; evidence reports; MEP opinions)
Force of law Yes — mandatory No — advisory, recommendations only
Key words "must," "shall," "required" "consider," "may," "should," "could"
ME may decline to use? No Yes — ME free to choose

FMCSA expects MEs to individually assess each driver against the regulatory standards; guidance may be considered; ME makes the final determination. Standards built from evidence reports, expert opinion, Medical Review Board; open to public comment.

ME certification and continued listing (49 CFR 390 Subpart D)

Eligibility: MD, DO, DC, APRN/NP, PA, or other professional authorized by state law to perform physicals; complete FMCSA-compliant training; register on National Registry (10-digit number); pass exam at FMCSA-approved testing org (passing 71%).

Requirement Number

Recertification (retake training + pass exam) Every 10 years (in 9th or 10th year)
Report profile changes (address, phone, email, license expiration) Within 30 days
Review NR profile Periodic review (quarterly or better) is a [training] recommendation — the regulatory duty is the 30-day change report

Audits: random, for cause, or eligibility (2024 enhanced monitoring). NOPR via NR account → acknowledge within 30 days → complete corrective actions within 60 days → confirm in account; failure = removal.

Appendix A to Part 391 Medical Advisory Criteria (guidance)

13 standards — 391.41(b)

# Standard Discretion
1 No loss of foot/leg/hand/arm — or SPE certificate D
2 No hand/finger impairment affecting prehension/power grasp; no limb impairment/defect interfering with driving — or SPE D
3 No insulin-treated DM unless meets 391.46 D
4 No current dx of MI, angina, coronary insufficiency, thrombosis, or CV disease with syncope, dyspnea, collapse, CHF D
5 No respiratory dysfunction likely to interfere D
6 No high BP likely to interfere D
7 No rheumatic/arthritic/orthopedic/muscular/neuromuscular/vascular disease that interferes D
8 No epilepsy or condition likely to cause LOC/loss of control Non-discretionary
9 No mental/nervous/organic/functional/psychiatric disorder that interferes D
10 Vision: 20/40 each eye (corrected OK), binocular 20/40, 70° horizontal field each eye, recognize red/green/amber; else 391.44 Non-discretionary
11 Hearing: forced whisper at ≥5 ft (aid OK) OR better-ear average loss ≤40 dB at 500/1,000/2,000 Hz (ANSI Z24.5-1951) Non-discretionary
12 No Schedule I drug, amphetamine, narcotic, habit-forming drug; other scheduled drugs only if prescribed by licensed practitioner familiar with history who advises no effect on safe driving D
13 No current clinical diagnosis of alcoholism D

Non-discretionary = objective criteria; failing = must not certify. Other 10 = ME clinical judgment.

Exemption programs

Program Status ME action
Hearing exemption Current (interstate only) Certify "otherwise qualified," max 2 yrs; mark "accompanied by" exemption, write "hearing"; do not also check "wearing hearing aid." Driver carries exemption + MEC
Seizure exemption Current Epilepsy dx: max 1 yr; single unprovoked seizure: max 2 yrs; mark "accompanied by federal seizure exemption"
Vision exemption Gone — replaced by 391.44 Alternative Vision Standard Annual cert; MCSA-5871 within 45 days
Diabetes exemption Gone — replaced by 391.46 MCSA-5870; 12-month cert
Grandfathered programs Expired —
SPE certificate (391.49) Current; not an exemption — alternative standard for fixed limb deficit Physiatrist/orthopedist review → FMCSA issues; valid up to 2 yrs, renew 30 days before expiry; driver carries SPE + MEC

Waiver = state-specific (intrastate); exemption = federal, FMCSA-granted. ME determines only whether driver is "otherwise qualified."

MER determination options (federal section)

Does not meet standards (explain, discuss, report to FMCSA) · Meets standards, 2-yr cert · Meets standards, periodic monitoring required (reason + period) · Check boxes: wearing corrective lenses / wearing hearing aid / accompanied by waiver-exemption (specify) / driving within exempt intracity zone (391.62) / accompanied by SPE certificate · Determination pending · Incomplete exam. Never use "pending" for a condition that interferes with safe driving. [training] Second opinion after disqualification requires employer permission; second ME gets full history and is told it is a second opinion.

NRII rule (eff. June 23, 2025): MEC sent electronically NR → SDLA for CDL/CLP; 5 states not fully implemented (AK, CA, KY, LA, NH); FMCSA still recommends paper MECs; paper MEC valid as proof up to 60 days after issue (through Oct 11, 2026). Non-CDL drivers always get paper. Intrastate results need not be uploaded.

ME role and obligations

  • No diagnosing, prescribing, or specific medical advice; exam = public safety determination (interference with safe driving, or risk of incapacitation/sudden death).
  • Do: thorough exam of all 13-standard systems; document on MER; consider meds/treatments; follow regs, consider guidance; request testing/specialist eval when unsure; educate on med side effects, labels, seeking care.
  • Must disqualify: fails non-discretionary standard; public safety risk; permanent restriction interfering with driving. May disqualify: fails guidelines/advisory criteria, uncertain clinical course. Possible sudden incapacitation → get treating-provider info/refer before deciding.
  • Consulting treating provider: driver consent + release; ask for actual records/results, not just recommendations; retain with MER; decision remains ME's.
  • Intrastate-only drivers: follow state regs (may be stricter; know state waivers); if in doubt, use federal. Municipal and exempt intracity zone drivers may be exempt from federal standards unless state/employer requires. Domicile = SDLA's concern, not ME's.
  • Motor carriers may not influence ME decisions or request MEC limitations.
  • Anyone may receive a DOT physical; age 21, English, road test, single CDL = motor carrier responsibility (391.11).
  • Certify for the most demanding CMV job, not the driver's current route.
  • Office: no FMCSA facility mandate; Snellen at 20 ft; 5-ft quiet space for whisper; [training] driver changes into an exam gown for the physical.

Driver responsibilities

Comply with 391.41; recertify before MEC expires or when health status changes; use NR-listed ME (carrier may specify one); photo ID; accurate, complete, signed health history on MER (surgeries, all meds incl. OTC/supplements, 32 conditions, prior denial/short cert); supply treating-clinician info on request; CDL drivers give MEC (some states also MER) to SDLA — not required of non-CDL.

Privacy / PHI

  • HIPAA (1996) Privacy Rule (HHS OCR); comply with federal + state law. Rule: no PHI disclosure without HIPAA-compliant consent.
  • Without consent OK: serious and imminent threat (45 CFR 164.512(j)); MEC to current/prospective employer on request; records to FMCSA/federal/state/local law enforcement within 48 h (391.43(i)); complaint to FMCSA alleging "substantial violation" (49 CFR 386.12(a)).
  • Employer who paid gets MEC on request, not the MER without consent.

Job of commercial driving

  • Profile [training]: typically male, >40, sedentary, overweight, smoker, >2 medical conditions, CV disease prevalent; overall health poorer than the general population.
  • HOS (49 CFR 395): 14-h daily work, 11-h max driving, 60 h/7 days or 70 h/8 days, log for last 7 days.
  • Tasks [legacy]: mirror checks demand neck rotation; a large steering wheel requiring sustained grip and steering control; enter/exit with 3 points of contact, hip/knee flexion >90°; coupling (grip, upper body, ROM, balance); loading >6.0 METs; the regulations specify no lifting weight.

High-yield points

  • The non-discretionary standards are vision, hearing, and epilepsy — the three with objective pass/fail criteria; HTN and diabetes are discretionary.
  • Only two federal exemption programs remain: seizure and hearing. The ME never grants exemptions; grandfathered programs have ended.
  • The MER is the mandatory exam documentation; retain 3 years. It is not routinely transmitted anywhere — an employer sees it only with driver consent.
  • MEC for CDL/CLP drivers is submitted to the National Registry (→ SDLA); non-CDL drivers receive paper. The original belongs to the driver; the ME keeps a copy 3 yrs; a MEC is issued only when the driver qualifies.
  • Guidance is advisory; the ME may decline to follow it. FMCSA expects an individual assessment of each driver against the regulatory standards; consulting the advisory criteria, a specialist, or the treating provider is at the ME's discretion.
  • Interstate vs intrastate turns on crossing state lines / the shipment's origin and destination; vehicle weight, hazmat, and passenger count define whether a vehicle is a CMV, not whether the operation is interstate.
  • Federal rules touching drivers' health: medical certification, drug/alcohol screening, hours of service. Routine ECGs and screening audiograms are not federally required.
  • The carrier verifies age ≥21, English proficiency, valid MEC, a single CDL, and the road test; the regulations set no minimum lifting requirement.
  • Age is no bar to the exam: an under-21 applicant is examined as usual (age 21 is the carrier's hiring concern).
  • Max certification 2 years, never extended; shorter always allowed. Carrier-requested MEC limitations are prohibited.
  • Attire [training]: driver gowned for the physical.
  • Disqualifying by regulation: current dx of alcoholism (b)(13); epilepsy (b)(8). Most other chronic findings (elevated BP, past arthritis, non-insulin DM, obesity, myopia) call for individual assessment, not automatic disqualification.
  • Insulin-treated DM can be certified when 391.46 is met — the pathway is the regulation itself, and it imposes no waiting period.
  • ME duties include health education/counseling; diagnosis, prescribing, and treatment plans belong to treating providers. The exam screens for conditions creating an unacceptable public safety risk.
  • Continued listing requires the 5-yr refresher, 10-yr recert, current license, and 30-day profile updates. There is no minimum exam count (zero-exam months are simply reported) and no annual CE-hour requirement.
  • "Treating clinician" = prescribes insulin; "treating provider" = coordinates care.
  • Intrastate-only driver → know and follow state regs.
  • Records may go to federal/state/local law enforcement without consent (48-hour rule). The MER reaches an employer — even one who paid for the exam — only with driver consent, and exam results are not shared with a prospective employer or PCP without it. HIPAA still applies; FMCSA regs do not override it.
  • CMV drivers as a population carry a heavier illness burden than average; typical driver is over 40; max 11 h driving per shift.
  • Cognitive demands of the job: problem solving, communication, judgment, appropriate behavior. Stressors: long relay routes, abrupt schedule changes, responsibility for passenger safety.
  • The ME alone determines fitness for duty.
