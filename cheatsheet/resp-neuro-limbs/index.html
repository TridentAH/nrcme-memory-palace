Respiratory, Neuromuscular & Limbs

RESPIRATORY — 49 CFR 391.41(b)(5)

Standard: no established medical history or clinical diagnosis of respiratory dysfunction likely to interfere with safe CMV operation. Advisory criteria: (1) O2-exchange conditions — emphysema, chronic asthma, carcinoma, TB, chronic bronchitis, OSA; (2) suspected undiagnosed/inadequately treated dysfunction → confer with treating provider or refer to specialist. ME's primary goal: establish whether disease increases risk of sudden death or incapacitation.

Exam findings → pulmonary eval/consult: clubbing, cyanosis, prolonged expiration, tachypnea at rest, wheezes/rhonchi/rales, absent/decreased breath sounds, pleural rub, unequal R/L thorax inflation, significant thoracic kyphosis/scoliosis, accessory muscle use at rest.

PFTs and thresholds

Test Indicated when Threshold
PFTs Lung disease history; SOB/cough/chest tightness/wheeze; cigarette smoker ≥35 y —
Spirometry (FEV1, FVC) Lung disease history or symptoms (not cor pulmonale, allergic rhinitis, or suspected OSA) Asthma: no cert if FEV1 < 65%; abnormal → further eval before cert
Pulse oximetry Condition causing airway obstruction Normal 95–100%; request ABG if < 92%
ABG Airway obstruction May not meet standard: PaO2 < 65 mm Hg (< 5,000 ft), < 50 mm Hg (> 5,000 ft), or PaCO2 > 45 mm Hg

Oxygen therapy: supplemental O2 needed for exertion → disqualify (even with pulmonologist note). Hypoxemia-at-rest clue: capillary refill > 2 s [training].

*Certify only after etiology confirmed and treatment proven effective, safe, stable. Lung transplant not addressed in sources.

Medications

  • 1st-gen antihistamines (diphenhydramine): sedating up to 12 h → defer cert pending PCP consult for non-sedating alternative. 2nd-gen (fexofenadine): most don't interfere.
  • Montelukast concern: ear pain/trouble hearing → hearing standard (b)(11) [training]. Clinically, montelukast's headline warning is neuropsychiatric (FDA boxed warning).
  • Hydrocodone (Vicodin) for cough: Schedule II, half-life 4 h, needs (b)(12)(ii) prescription exception → request prescriber info (MCSA-5895).
  • Pirbuterol/albuterol listed → ask why (asthma?). Chronic cough for months on OTC meds → refer to specialist.

Obstructive Sleep Apnea (replaces Jan 2015 Bulletin)

  • Regulations do not require screening, referral for testing, specific diagnostic/treatment methods, compliance assessment, waiting periods, or max cert periods. Further guidance: Nov 21, 2016 advisory recommendations (MRB Task 16-01). No CPAP hours/% nights criteria in sources.
  • Treated moderate-to-severe OSA does not preclude. Key: treatment effective, safe, stable.
  • FMCSA favors multiple risk factors (not one criterion): small airway history, loud snoring, witnessed apneas, sleepiness during major wake periods, obesity/high BMI, large neck, HTN, CVD, stroke/diabetes/comorbidities. Multiple factors → consider sleep study/board-certified sleep specialist unless previously evaluated. Prior negative/mild study → no repeat unless significant change. No routine retesting; treating provider decides.
  • AHI: apnea = no airflow > 10 s; hypopnea = ≥30% flow drop ≥10 s + O2 drop > 3–4%. Mild ≥5/h, moderate ≥15/h, severe >30/h.
  • Optional screening (AASM-style screen commonly taught in training courses): history — HTN needing >2 meds, T2DM, AF/nocturnal dysrhythmia, CHF, stroke, pulm HTN, drowsy MVAs, bariatric candidate. Findings — high Berlin/Epworth/STOP-BANG score, neck > 17 in men / > 16 in women, Mallampati 3–4, retrognathia, macroglossia, large uvula, narrow palate, nasal abnormalities, BMI > 35.
  • Actual Nov 2016 FMCSA MRB (Task 16-01) recommendation: refer for sleep study at BMI ≥ 40, or BMI 33–40 plus ≥3 risk factors; neck > 17 in (male) / > 15.5 in (female).

NEUROMUSCULAR — 49 CFR 391.41(b)(7)

Standard: no established history/diagnosis of rheumatic, arthritic, orthopedic, muscular, neuromuscular, or vascular disease interfering with safe operation. Advisory criteria: diagnosis = established history; consider nature/severity, degree of limitation (ROM), rate/stage of progression, interference; severe functional impairment = not qualified; may certify < max period for monitoring.

  • Certification covers driving AND non-driving/heavy labor, every employer and vehicle type. ME may not add restrictions (e.g., lifting limits) — employer's discretion; unable to do normal tasks → disqualify.
  • Job demands: unload up to 50,000 lb after prolonged sitting; tire chains 35–90 lb; tarp 50–100 lb; steering needs power grasp + prehension; gear shift = right arm/hand + left lower leg; backing needs depth perception. Driving is the least demanding part.
  • Exam: equilibrium/coordination, speech, DTRs, patellar/Babinski, sensory/positional, ataxia, alertness/comprehension (alcohol/drug history not part of this portion). No test required — grip test (dynamometer/sphygmomanometer) optional; specialist referral at ME discretion.

Spinal cord injury, joint replacement, fusion, amputation, ortho surgery: no waiting periods in sources — case-by-case; fixed limb deficit → SPE route; spine/torso → no SPE.

LIMBS — 391.41(b)(1), (b)(2), 391.49

Rule Text Key
(b)(1) Loss No loss of foot, leg, hand, arm — OR SPE Hand loss = all 5 fingers. Loss precludes without SPE even with prosthesis. Visualize all extremities
(b)(2) Impairment No hand/finger impairment interfering with prehension or power grasp; no arm/foot/leg or other limb defect interfering with normal tasks — OR SPE < 5 fingers or any toes → (b)(2) functional eval. SPE unavailable for a spine/torso impairment that does not result in a limb impairment (391.49); a spine problem causing a fixed limb deficit (e.g., spinal stenosis w/ foot drop) is evaluated on the limb
391.49 Fails (b)(1)/(b)(2) but otherwise qualified → may drive if FMCSA grants SPE Eligible under (b)(1), (b)(2), or both

SPE facts

  • Fixed deficit only (stable over lifetime); not progressive disease (MS). Fixed status judged by board-qualified/certified physiatrist or orthopedic surgeon; FMCSA makes final decision and issues (only FMCSA grants SPEs/exemptions).
  • FMCSA may require demonstration to an agent: pre-trip/non-driving, off-highway, on-highway — not always required.
  • Valid up to 2 yr; renew 30 days before expiration; may restrict prosthetic/equipment use. Driver carries SPE + MEC while driving.
  • Hand/arm loss: prosthesis must allow precision prehension (knobs/switches, thumb+fingers) and power grasp prehension (steering wheel, whole hand). Current 391.49: demonstrate both with each hand separately; if not, fitted with prosthesis/orthotic before applying (post–Jan 6, 1986 drivers).
  • Do not certify: impairment of torso; no proof of SPE compliance; limb impairment from progressive disease.

ME role

  1. SPE presented → proceed with exam; evaluate unaffected limbs normally.
  2. Address "Yes"/"Not sure" answers; answer question 19; mark question 11 (extremities/joints) Abnormal; document location/extent, how/when, prosthesis, interference — specific enough for FMCSA.
  3. Determination: "Meets Standards" + "Accompanied by SPE certificate" on MCSA-5875 and MCSA-5876 (federal option; complete federal OR state section only).
  4. Give driver MER + MEC for application. PCP clearance not required.
  5. Max cert with SPE = 2 yr (e.g., BKA + SPE → 2 yr). The federal seizure exemption is the one program keyed to annual certification [course — hearing-exemption terms also involve periodic recertification].

Musculoskeletal exam

  • Neck ROM (mirrors/backing), spine, extremities (brace, atrophy, deformity, leg length), gait, pedal strength, shoulder/elbow, precision + power grasp. No required ROM values or grip test. Ask about previous spine/musculoskeletal surgery, muscle-relaxant use, torso pain.
  • Optional: railing grab, steering/gear simulation, lift known weights, one-leg balance, cab entry, box-and-block/nine-hole peg.
  • Wrist fusion, grip 12 lb R vs 65 lb L → disqualify.

High-yield points

  • COPD needing supplemental O2 for exertion → disqualify, even with a pulmonologist's note.
  • Active asthma flare — wheezing, rescue inhaler several times a day → disqualify and refer for pulmonary care; there is no short-term certificate for an uncontrolled flare.
  • COPD smoker, productive cough, SOB on exam → disqualify pending pulmonary evaluation; the referral comes before ordering an ABG yourself.
  • 2nd spontaneous pneumothorax same side, no surgery, FVC 66% → defer pending pulmonary eval. Recent pneumothorax → first investigate symptoms/resolution.
  • Significant thoracic scoliosis → pulmonary consult. Of the respiratory diagnoses, emphysema most warrants specialist evaluation.
  • Pulse ox/ABG are indicated by conditions causing airway obstruction; spirometry by lung-disease history or symptoms such as chest tightness/wheezing.
  • Sedating meds last 12 h; Benadryl → defer pending PCP.
  • Hypersensitivity pneumonitis → certifiable; the driver must avoid the causative agent, including transporting it. TB history alone → not precluded. Primary alveolar hypoventilation → unlikely ever to meet the standard.
  • Anaphylaxis history → key = successful preventive measures.
  • OSA: weigh multiple risk factors, never one alone; obese HTN snorer w/ witnessed apneas → refer to sleep specialist; sleepy obese driver, crowded airway, recent drowsy-driving crash → disqualify + refer; new CPAP → confirm treatment effective, safe, stable.
  • No required grip test; severe functional impairment = not qualified. Isaac's → most likely precludes; MS/PD/neuropathy/muscular dystrophy → case-by-case; ALS/MG/metabolic muscle disease → disqualifying [legacy].
  • The ME cannot add a lifting restriction for an employer; a driver who can't do the normal tasks is disqualified.
  • Carpal tunnel or missing 2 fingers → test grip/prehension first. Only fixed limb deficit eligible for SPE.
  • SPE: 2 yr, renew 30 days prior, FMCSA grants, no torso, demo not always required, driver carries it, ME proceeds with exam, max cert 2 yr.
  • Heavy-labor fitness is about ROM, strength, and balance; sustained attention belongs to the neurological/psychological evaluation.
