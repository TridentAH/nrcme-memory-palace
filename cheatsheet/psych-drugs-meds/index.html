Psychological, Drugs & Alcohol, Medications

PSYCHOLOGICAL — 49 CFR 391.41(b)(9)

Standard: no "mental, nervous, organic, or functional disease or psychiatric disorder likely to interfere with the ability to drive a CMV safely." Discretionary. Diagnosis alone never disqualifies — case-by-case on function and relevant history; the more serious the dx, the less likely to qualify.

Advisory criteria: (1) emotional/adjustment problems affect memory, reasoning, attention, judgment; often caused by physical disorders. (2) Most critical: ability to "appreciate, evaluate, and adequately respond to environmental strain and emotional stress." (3) Highly susceptible to frequent emotional instability (schizophrenia, affective psychoses, paranoia, severe anxiety, depressive neuroses) → unlikely to meet standard. (4) Consider medication side effects/interactions.

Assess 3 areas: the disorder itself · residual symptoms after episodes · pharmacological effects of treatment.

Mental status / assessment elements

  • Review MER (MCSA-5875); discuss every "yes." Ask: nervous/psychiatric disorder; altered/loss of consciousness; psych meds; frequent alcohol; narcotic/habit-forming drugs.
  • Observe: dress; suspicious/evasive/hostile/threatening behavior; distraction; flat affect; bizarre ideas; hallucinations; dishonesty/omissions.
  • Ask: hopeless/depressed (how often); memory/concentration; self-harm thoughts; suicide attempt; fights; harming others; psychiatric hospitalization; work/driving/military/legal/social history. (Tinnitus belongs to the hearing work-up, not the psych history.)
  • Uncooperative driver / exam cannot be completed → disqualify.
Test Use
MMSE Cognitive screen; risk for dementia/cognitive impairment
Neuropsychological testing Verbal, memory, cognition, motor; aids dx of Parkinson's, TBI
Personality tests (MMPI most common) Clarify dx, guide treatment

Document: onset & dx; med dose/frequency; limitations; potential side effects (incl. OTC); abnormal findings + effect on driving + corrective steps; testing requested. MCSA-5895 CMV Driver Medication Form: optional, voluntary, driver consent, to treating/prescribing provider.

Condition tables (Appendix A)

Condition Waiting period Max cert Do NOT certify if
Mood disorders (major depression, bipolar) 6 mo symptom-free after non-psychotic major depression without suicidal behavior; 1 yr symptom-free after severe depressive episode, suicide attempt, or manic episode 1 yr Active psychosis; side effects interfering; prominent negative sx (compromised judgment, attentional difficulties, suicidal thoughts/behavior, overt inappropriate acts)
Schizophrenia & related psychotic disorders 6 mo symptom-free after brief reactive psychosis or schizophreniform; 1 yr symptom-free other psychotic disorders 1 yr Diagnosis of schizophrenia; active psychosis; negative sx; side effects
Adult ADHD None* 1 yr Active psychosis; side effects; negative sx
Antisocial/personality disorders None* 1 yr Same; PD "repeatedly manifested by overt, inappropriate acts"

May certify (all): complies with treatment; tolerates without disqualifying side effects (sedation, impaired coordination); comprehensive eval by mental health professional who understands CMV driving. Not until etiology confirmed and treatment effective, safe, and stable*.

  • Bipolar: ≥1 manic episode ± depression. Mania: irritable/expansive/elevated mood, ↓ judgment, ↑ substance abuse, ± delusions/hallucinations. Tx: lithium or anticonvulsant; antipsychotic if psychosis; antidepressants for bipolar depression.
  • Major depression: depressed/irritable mood, anhedonia, withdrawal, sleep/appetite change, fatigue, restlessness/agitation, ↓ memory/concentration, poor judgment, suicidality; psychosis in severe cases. Most recover; some relapse within 5 yr; stressors in prior 6 mo. Hallucinations belong to psychosis, not to a routine depressive episode.
  • Schizophrenia: most severe psychotic disorder — psychosis, negative sx, impaired cognition/judgment/attention, ↑ suicide. Related: schizophreniform, brief reactive psychosis, schizoaffective, delusional. Antipsychotic side effects worst at initiation/dose change.
  • PTSD: anxiety disorder after trauma; mood swings, suicidal thoughts. Exposure therapy (CBT/cognitive processing); mild–moderate treated in ~1 yr. Meds almost always + psychotherapy: SSRIs; benzos → dependence, may worsen PTSD; antipsychotics, mood stabilizers. Key Q: psychiatrist/psychologist eval & recommendations.
  • Adult ADHD: age-inappropriate impulsiveness/hyperactivity; mood lability, explosiveness, low frustration tolerance; comorbid antisocial/borderline PD, substance abuse. Before certifying on stimulant: request treating-provider eval, verify adult ADHD dx, determine side effects; check opioid/benzo interactions.
  • Bipolar, ADHD, personality disorders all ↑ substance-abuse risk.
  • Dementia: not disqualifying by regulation; cognitive deficits (memory, planning, complex tasks, awareness) mean driver may lack ability; majority Alzheimer's; incurable.
  • Anxiety/panic: no separate table; "severe anxiety" in criterion 3; benzos → prescription exception. Eating disorders: not addressed in source.

Psychiatric therapies

Therapy Wait Max cert Key points
Antidepressants None* 1 yr Case-by-case. First-generation TCAs (amitriptyline/Elavil, clomipramine, amoxapine, doxepin, imipramine, nortriptyline) → more side effects (dizziness, drowsiness, ↓ alertness); Appendix A: should not certify first-gen user. Second-gen (SSRIs, norepinephrine reuptake modulators, unicyclic aminoketones) fewer effects but can still impair; tolerance to sedation develops. MAOIs listed only, no specific rule. Esketamine (Spravato) = Sched III. SSRIs not controlled → focus = history & functioning.
Antipsychotics None* 1 yr Typical/atypical neuroleptics; only partially correct sx. Side effects: sedation, visual disturbances (esp. night), motor dysfunction, arrhythmia, orthostatic hypotension. Also for nausea, chronic pain. Use not disqualifying itself. Lurasidone not controlled; dizziness/syncope.
Anxiolytics / sedative-hypnotics None* 2 yr Benzos impair; non-benzos less; barbiturates worst. Both Sched II–V → prescription exception. May certify: short-acting hypnotic (half-life < 5 h), lowest dose, < 2 weeks, or non-sedating anxiolytic. Do NOT certify sedating anxiolytic. Diazepam's half-life runs roughly 24–48 h (active metabolites longer) [clinical], so it lingers for days after the last dose; first Q = why prescribed (alcohol withdrawal ⇒ current alcoholism). Ambien: next-day impairment.
CNS stimulants (dextroamphetamine, methylphenidate, pemoline; Adderall/Vyvanse Sched II) — — Narcolepsy, adult ADHD, adjunct to antidepressants; low doses ↑ vigilance/simple tasks, not complex. Controlled → request prescriber info (MCSA-5895); not automatically disqualifying.
ECT 6 mo symptom-free after course — Acute confusion, disorientation, short-term memory loss; resolves within months. Certify if waiting period done, mental-health eval, not on maintenance ECT.
Lithium None* — Bipolar & depressive disorders; little evidence it impairs driving. Certify if asymptomatic and levels in therapeutic range. Side effects: drowsiness, dizziness, fatigue, fine tremor.
  • Regulatory max for a history of a psychological disorder = 2 years (391.45 — no psych-specific interval; ME decides). The legacy App-A tables recommended 1 yr for most conditions; training courses key 2 years.
  • Anticonvulsant mood stabilizers: carbamazepine, lamotrigine, valproate, divalproex. Pregabalin (Lyrica) = Schedule V.

DRUG USE — 49 CFR 391.41(b)(12)

Part Rule
(b)(12)(i) Does not use any drug in 21 CFR 1308.11 Schedule I, an amphetamine, a narcotic, or any other habit-forming drug.
(b)(12)(ii) "prescription exception" Does not use any non-Schedule I drug (21 CFR 1308) except when prescribed by a licensed medical practitioner who is licensed to prescribe controlled substances, is familiar with the driver's medical history and job duties, and has advised the driver the drug will not adversely affect safe CMV operation.

Advisory criteria: (1) Schedule I cannot be prescribed for any purpose → ME cannot qualify, under any circumstances. (2) ME may qualify amphetamine/narcotic/Schedules II–V (1308.12–1308.15) if exception met. (3) Written communication from the prescriber shows exception met; MCSA-5895 = voluntary tool (driver consent). (4) ME may request a non-DOT drug test (e.g., signs of undisclosed use); no SAP required (49 CFR 40.3, 40.281); may ask for drug-use assessment by qualified professional.

  • ME makes the final decision; may disqualify even if prescriber says safe. Based on history + exam + testing ME deems necessary.
  • No standard for non-scheduled drugs — evaluate by underlying condition and side effects (vision, drowsiness, dizziness, confusion, reaction time, mood).
  • No mandatory waiting period after disclosed illegal use; prior use doesn't preclude; ME may withhold MEC pending assessment/treatment/testing/counseling. Evasive driver, refuses non-DOT test, can't rule out current use → disqualify.
  • Intoxication signs at exam (fresh injection marks, pinpoint pupils, unsteady gait) → disqualify; don't let him drive away. Dilated pupils, HR 116, clammy skin, vague history → request non-DOT test.
  • Ask: therapeutic vs habitual; whether the driver uses drugs to change how he feels or to push past normal limits; rehab history; noncompliance risk; monitoring; interactions.

Marijuana / CBD

  • Marijuana & extracts > 0.3% THC = Schedule I. Positive test/use → disqualified even in states legalizing recreational, medical, or religious use; "medically prescribed" marijuana → disqualify.
  • CBD < 0.3% THC: not automatically disqualifying; no federal labeling oversight → may cause positive, disqualifying test. CBD gummies → discuss risks. Guidance: DOT Office of Drug and Alcohol Policy; FDA.

Opioid-agonist treatment

Drug Sched Rule
Methadone II Does NOT automatically preclude certification; need prescriber info (familiar w/ history & duties) that tx won't adversely affect driving; final decision = ME. Driver a few months into a methadone program → consult the prescribing practitioner.
Suboxone (buprenorphine/naloxone) III Same — not automatically disqualifying; prescription exception; request prescriber info (MCSA-5895).

DOT drug & alcohol testing — 49 CFR 382 (employer's program, not the ME's)

  • CDL/CLP holders operating CMV on public roads: GVWR > 26,000 lb, 16 or more passengers (incl. driver), or placarded hazmat. Includes government, owner-operators, for-hire, Mexican/Canadian-licensed drivers.
  • 5 substances: marijuana, cocaine, amphetamines, opioids, PCP (not alcohol, not nicotine).
  • Required pre-employment; also random, reasonable suspicion, post-accident, return-to-duty, follow-up. Pre-employment alcohol test = employer option, not a DOT requirement.
  • Separate from physical exam; may be concurrent. Drug testing is the motor carrier's responsibility. Oversight: DOT Office of Drug and Alcohol Policy and Compliance.
  • MER Qs 31 & 32 (illegal drug use) support (b)(12) — not meant to involve ME in Part 40/382 testing.
  • 382.213: (a) no Schedule I on safety-sensitive duty; (b) non-Schedule I only w/ prescriber advice; (c) employer w/ actual knowledge must not permit; (d) employer may require drivers to report therapeutic drug use.
  • 392.4: on duty, no possession/use/influence of Schedule I, amphetamines ("pep pills, bennies"), narcotics, or any incapacitating substance; carriers may not permit; practitioner-administered excepted.
  • Clearinghouse: database of CDL/CLP drug/alcohol violations; employers, MROs, SAPs must report; since Nov 18, 2024 SDLAs check before issuing/renewing/upgrading/transferring CDL/CLP and downgrade "prohibited" drivers until return-to-duty done. Doesn't directly affect MEs.
  • Source gives no cutoffs, BAC thresholds, or SAP process details.

DEA schedules (Appendix B)

Sched Definition Examples
I No accepted medical use; high abuse; illegal Heroin, LSD, marijuana, peyote, MDMA, methaqualone, psilocybin, designer benzos
II Accepted use, high abuse → "dangerous," most strictly regulated; never OTC Amphetamine (Adderall), methylphenidate, Vyvanse, oxycodone, hydrocodone, fentanyl, morphine, methadone, meperidine, codeine, pentobarbital, secobarbital, PCP
III Moderate–low; psychological > physical dependence Tylenol #3 (≤ 90 mg codeine), buprenorphine/Suboxone, anabolic steroids, testosterone, ketamine, esketamine, dronabinol, Xyrem, benzphetamine
IV Limited dependence Xanax, Klonopin, Valium, Ativan, temazepam, triazolam, Ambien, orexin antagonists, carisoprodol (Soma), tramadol, modafinil (Provigil), pentazocine
V Least; antidiarrheal/antitussive/analgesic Lomotil, Motofen, Robitussin AC, Phenergan w/ codeine, pregabalin, ezogabine

Most misused: opioids, depressants, stimulants; most common reason = physical pain (2016 NSDUH; >10 million/yr).

ALCOHOLISM — 49 CFR 391.41(b)(13)

  • Qualified if "no current clinical diagnosis of alcoholism." "Current" = current alcoholic illness or condition not fully stabilized, regardless of time involved.
  • ME may certify prior dx in remission; residual neurological changes evaluated under the applicable standard (alcoholic neuropathy → (b)(7)).
  • ME determines current alcoholism (exam + driver info + testing). May order non-DOT alcohol test (excessive use disclosed or signs seen); no SAP required; may require professional assessment (current dx vs remission).
  • "Yes" to frequent alcohol → first characterize how much, how often, and in what pattern the driver drinks. Signs: tremor, liver enlargement, cognitive impairment.
  • Fresh out of court-ordered rehab after a DUI, still in early recovery, no professional assessment yet → active alcoholism → disqualify (may refer to SAP).
  • Disulfiram (Antabuse) ⇒ current alcoholism → precludes certification. Other FDA alcohol meds: acamprosate, naltrexone. Valium for withdrawal ⇒ ineligible.
  • Impairment via intoxication and withdrawal, even off-duty use.
Tool Detail
AUDIT WHO, 10 items; score ≥ 8 = harmful/hazardous use [clinical]
CAGE Cut down, Annoyed, Guilty, Eye-opener; ≥ 2 "yes" suggests dependence [clinical — FMCSA mandates neither screen]

Alcoholism in remission (App. A): wait none (after completing counseling/treatment); max 2 yr; certify if no residual impairment, treatment completed, no current alcohol disorder. Do not certify: current dx; signs of illness or noncompliance w/ DOT alcohol guidelines; unstable alcohol-related condition; return-to-duty not met.

High-yield points

  • (b)(9) turns on whether a disorder is likely to interfere — there is no bar on history alone, on taking psychiatric medication, and no fixed year count.
  • Lithium = bipolar & depressive disorders; monitor therapeutic level. CNS stimulants treat narcolepsy/ADHD, are scheduled, do NOT preclude. Anxiolytics treat anxiety + insomnia.
  • Past psychosis → case-by-case. Chronic schizophrenia is "unlikely to meet the standard" — advisory language, not a regulatory disqualification; with active psychosis, certification is effectively out. Neither antipsychotic use nor dementia is disqualifying by regulation. The schizophreniform 6-month wait is App-A guidance, not a mandate (training courses key "no mandated wait").
  • Regulatory max for a psych-disorder history = 2 years (391.45); the legacy App-A tables recommended 1 yr for most conditions — training courses key 2 years.
  • TCAs (amitriptyline, clomipramine, amoxapine) → assess individually; the side effect that matters most for driving is dizziness; amoxapine is not a controlled substance.
  • An SSRI-treated driver who is visibly distracted and can't focus shows disqualifying attentional signs → disqualify & refer. A driver nearly a year past a suicide attempt, cleared and euthymic → the remaining question is medication side effects. Citalopram prescribed for GAD → what matters is history & current functioning.
  • Adderall/Vyvanse (II), Ativan/Valium/Ambien (IV), pregabalin/Lomotil (V), Suboxone (III), methadone (II) → the move is the prescription exception: request prescriber info / MCSA-5895, not reflexive disqualification or a fixed 1-yr cert.
  • Benzodiazepines (Xanax/Klonopin/Valium) sit in Schedule IV, well below the Schedule II opioids and stimulants. Tramadol = Sched IV. Naltrexone, tiotropium, sertraline, dicyclomine, varenicline, lurasidone = NOT controlled.
  • Medical marijuana / MDMA → disqualify; CBD gummies → discuss risks.
  • ME may order non-DOT tests; SAP not required; drug testing = motor carrier's job. Reasonable suspicion is a valid DOT trigger; anonymous tip is not. 5-panel excludes alcohol; rules = 49 CFR 382; pre-employment alcohol test optional.
  • Nicotine not prohibited; Quaalude Sched I; Schedule II = "dangerous." Employers may require reporting of therapeutic drug use.
  • Alcoholism dx = ME's call; CAGE-positive → non-DOT alcohol test; sober 10 yr + neuropathy → (b)(7); disulfiram → precludes.
  • Most common reason for drug misuse = physical pain (2016 NSDUH). Hostile or threatening behavior during the exam is itself a sign of possible psychiatric disorder; ECT's hallmark after-effects are confusion/disorientation; neuroleptics → visual disturbances (esp. at night); most critical assessment = response to environmental strain/emotional stress.
