Vision & Hearing

VISION — 49 CFR 391.41(b)(10)

Non-discretionary standard. Three requirements, all must be met:

Requirement Standard
Distant visual acuity ≥ 20/40 (Snellen) in each eye AND ≥ 20/40 binocular, with or without corrective lenses; record in Snellen-equivalent values
Field of vision ≥ 70° in the horizontal meridian of each eye
Color Recognize colors of traffic signals/devices showing red, green, amber
  • Fails any part, one or both eyes = not qualified. Near acuity NOT required.
  • Color: recognizing standard red/green/amber meets standard even with a color deficiency.
  • Testers: the ME, or an ophthalmologist/optometrist for the vision portion; [training] trained assistive personnel commonly perform the screening. ME makes final decision; signing MER attests to all results.

Corrective lenses / contacts / telescopic

  • Lenses used in testing must be worn driving; mark "wearing corrective lenses" on both MER (MCSA-5875) and MEC. Uncorrected acuity need not be documented if standard met corrected.
  • Contacts: verify tolerance/adaptation. Monovision (distance lens one eye, near lens other) acceptable if standard met.
  • Bioptic telescopic lenses: not permissible → disqualify. Advise spare glasses/contacts.

Alternative Vision Standard — 49 CFR 391.44 (Jan 2022; replaced FMCSA exemption requirement)

  • Monocular = worse eye fails the acuity standard even with corrective lenses, or fails the field standard (depth perception, contrast problems).
  • Better eye must meet: ≥ 20/40 Snellen; ≥ 70° horizontal; red/green/amber.
Step Detail
1. Specialist eval Licensed ophthalmologist or optometrist determines deficiency is stable and enough time has passed to adapt/compensate; completes, signs, dates Vision Evaluation Report MCSA-5871
2. Form validity 45 days — driver brings it to ME within 45 days
3. ME exam Driver must meet all other 391.41 standards; ME must consider MCSA-5871 info
4. Field testing (specialist) Formal perimetry required; modality testing ≥ 120° horizontal; results in degrees, copy of perimetry attached to MCSA-5871
5. Certificate Up to 12 months; re-exam & recert every 12 months
6. Road test Generally required for first-time AVS qualification; done after MEC issued and before interstate CMV operation; conducted by motor carrier per 49 CFR 391.31

Do NOT certify under AVS if: better eye < 20/40; better eye field < 70°; cannot recognize red/green/amber; deficiency not stable; insufficient time since stable.

When a driver fails vision screening

  • Disqualify; instruct to see ophthalmologist/optometrist. Do NOT use "Determination Pending" while the vision evaluation is being obtained.
  • If possibly AVS-eligible, have specialist complete MCSA-5871 → driver returns for a new physical qualification exam.
  • "Determination Pending" only after driver has had the specialist eval and provided the completed MCSA-5871; gives up to 45 days for clarification/additional info and to complete the exam.
  • Inconclusive peripheral screen → refer to ophthalmologist/optometrist for precise field testing (may then be AVS candidate).

Specialist reports

  • Required by regulation only for AVS. Report: name, phone, email, license no., state; signed/dated. Attach to MER, write "see attached documentation" in vision results.

Testing procedures

Test Key details
Snellen Chart 20 ft from driver (measure and mark); white light; numerator = 20 ft, denominator = last line read accurately; may skip to smaller lines; corrective lenses allowed; illiterate "E" chart OK; Titmus Vision Screener acceptable (acuity, depth, color, binocular)
Confrontation fields Examiner and driver sit facing about 2 ft apart, eyes level; each covers the mirror-opposite eye and the driver fixates on the examiner's open eye; examiner extends arms and brings wiggling fingers in from the periphery, halfway between the two, testing above and below eye level; driver reports when movement is seen; repeat for the other eye. Finger-count variant along horizontal field, one eye closed at a time
Color Standard test (Ishihara, Pseudoisochromatic, Yarn, Farnsworth) or controlled test (standard colors on Snellen chart, or red/green/amber objects). Fails Ishihara → next step is a controlled test, not disqualification

History & physical

  • Ask: vision changes, night vision, cataracts/glaucoma/retinopathy/macular degeneration, ophthalmic meds.
  • Exam: pupils, nystagmus, exophthalmos, light reaction, accommodation, motility, EOMs, fundoscopy. Note on MER: ptosis, discharge, abnormal field, color blindness, corneal scarring, uncorrected strabismus.
  • Some conditions fall under other standards (e.g., vascular disease, 391.41(b)(7)). ME not expected to diagnose → refer.

Conditions & meds

Condition Exam-relevant points
Diabetic retinopathy Insulin-treated DM + severe non-proliferative OR proliferative retinopathy = NOT qualified. Does not apply to non-insulin DM or retinopathy in general. Background retinopathy common after 5–7 yr DM. Affects contrast sensitivity, flicker fusion, color discrimination
Carcinoma-associated retinopathy Photoreceptor degeneration → rapid onset blindness
Cataracts Lens opacification; glare (night driving) early symptom; accelerated by injury, radiation, gout, steroids, DM; surgery + IOL; refer
Aphakia No lens; hyperopia (lost accommodation); risk glaucoma, retinal detachment; not automatically disqualifying
Glaucoma Abnormal IOP → optic nerve damage; painless; peripheral loss before symptoms; Snellen may be normal; loss irreversible; meds need strict compliance, may have side effects. Normal IOP 10–20 mmHg [clinical]
Macular degeneration Refer to ophthalmologist
Anisocoria Uneven pupils; often benign but may signal nervous system disorder, viral infection, stroke risk
CN III palsy Compression/ischemia; impaired eye movement, pupil reaction; diplopia in certain gazes, ptosis, dilated pupil → neurological evaluation
Nystagmus Repetitive uncontrolled movements; reduced vision/depth perception, night vision; symptom of another problem; worse with fatigue/stress
Strabismus Misalignment; diplopia, depth problems; note if uncorrected; not automatic DQ
Xanthelasma palpebrarum Benign cholesterol plaques on eyelids; hyperlipidemia, thyroid dysfunction, DM
Xanthopsia Yellow vision with digoxin
Floaters May indicate retinal tear/detachment → defer pending ophthalmology eval
Ophthalmic preparations Ask about side effects (stinging, burning, blurred vision, glare, reduced night vision, headaches, allergy); use may reveal disease (glaucoma). Check non-ophthalmic meds (antihistamines, anticholinergics)
Mydriatics Dilated exam drops (hydroxyamphetamine, tropicamide) used by specialists for retinal exam

Meets 391.41(b)(10) 2 years

HEARING — 49 CFR 391.41(b)(11)

Non-discretionary. Driver must EITHER: - First perceive a forced whispered voice in the better ear at not less than 5 feet, with or without hearing aid; OR - Audiometry: average hearing loss in the better ear not greater than 40 dB at 500, 1,000, 2,000 Hz, with or without hearing aid (or cochlear implant); device calibrated to ANSI Z24.5-1951.

  • Test both ears; only one (better) ear must pass.
  • Give one test first, complete both ears. Pass → no second test. Fail → must give the other test (single failed test insufficient to disqualify). Fails whisper both ears → audiometry.
  • Fails both tests → may see hearing specialist, then new physical qualification exam.

Forced whisper procedure

  • Quiet area, 5-ft distance; tested ear turned toward examiner, other ear covered; position out of the driver's view (no visual cues); using the breath that remains after a normal expiration, whisper words or random numbers (66, 18, 3); driver repeats. Avoid only sibilant words. Test both ears. Record distance (ft) first heard.

Audiometry

  • Record dB loss at 500/1,000/2,000 Hz per ear; average = sum ÷ 3. Pass if better ear ≤ 40 dB. 3,000 Hz NOT required.
  • ISO → ANSI conversion: subtract 14 dB (500 Hz), 10 dB (1,000 Hz), 8.5 dB (2,000 Hz), then average.
  • Worked example: R 28+34+36 = 98/3 = 32.7 pass; L 42+44+52 = 138/3 = 46 fail → qualified (one ear passes).
  • Audiologist report (name, phone, email, license, state; signed/dated) attached to MER; "see attached documentation." [training] Assistive personnel may test; ME signs and is responsible.

Hearing aids

  • Allowed for either test; office audiometers usually can't test with aids → audiologist.
  • Driver says aid not needed: a driver who passes unaided takes no aid restriction; if the unaided test fails, retest with the aid.
  • Qualifies with aid → must wear it (and keep it working) whenever driving; spare power source encouraged (2024 MEH) — [legacy] older guidance required carrying one, and many courses still teach "carry one." Cert up to 2 years. No exemption needed.
  • MER: the MCSA-5875 has only "Check if hearing aid used for test: Right Ear / Left Ear / Neither." MEC: check "Wearing a hearing aid."

Federal hearing exemption

Item Detail
Who Driver who does NOT meet standard even with hearing aids but meets all other 391.41(b)(1)–(13) standards
Scope Interstate commerce only
Decision FMCSA reviews the application and decides (apply: 202-366-4001, fmcsahearingexemptions@dot.gov, or online)
ME role Determine whether standard met with aids (whisper with aids, or audiologist audiometry with aids); complete full physical
ME cert Max 2 years
MER/MEC Check "Accompanied by a waiver/exemption," write "hearing." Do NOT also check "Wearing a hearing aid" — never both
Driver carries Current hearing exemption + current MEC
Responsibility for documentation Motor carrier

Ear exam

Check for hearing aid, ear disorders, canal and TM abnormalities. Scarred TM, other ear 5 ft → certify 2 years.

Hearing intervals

Driver Max interval
Meets standard (with or without aid) 2 years
Hearing exemption 2 years

High-yield points

  • The field standard is 70° per eye. A driver with a 62° field in one eye plus myopia/HTN → disqualify and refer (possible glaucoma).
  • Acuity below standard disqualifies outright — there is no 3-month certificate or road-test workaround for vision. Corrected 20/40 R + 20/60 L = disqualify (each eye must reach 20/40); corrected 20/25 R + 20/70 L with binocular 20/40 = disqualify. Cataract with corrected acuity of 20/100 → disqualify and refer (no temporary cert).
  • AVS: recert every year; MCSA-5871 within 45 days; ME must consider the form. AVS is an alternative standard in the regulation, not an FMCSA exemption; the eye specialist — not the driver — signs the MCSA-5871.
  • "Determination Pending" is correct only after the completed MCSA-5871 is in hand.
  • Bioptic telescopic lenses → disqualify regardless of a specialist's note.
  • Contacts irritating but driver passes with them → certify wearing the contacts (a spare pair doesn't cure intolerance; the ME doesn't prescribe).
  • Inconclusive confrontation field (e.g., after an orbital fracture) → refer for precise field testing first; AVS only if a deficiency is confirmed.
  • Fails Ishihara → administer a controlled color test next.
  • Self-limited surface conditions such as conjunctivitis need no specialist; retinopathy, cataract, glaucoma, and macular degeneration warrant referral.
  • The one automatic regulatory eye disqualification: insulin-treated DM + severe non-proliferative/proliferative retinopathy (391.46). Aphakia, dry eyes, retinopathy in general, strabismus, subconjunctival hemorrhage, and light sensitivity call for individual assessment.
  • Carcinoma-associated retinopathy can cause rapidly progressive blindness [clinical]. Glaucoma: painless, peripheral loss first, Snellen may stay normal [clinical].
  • New floaters → defer pending ophthalmology.
  • Hearing: whisper failed in both ears (e.g., 4 ft / 3 ft) → administer audiometry next; a driver may qualify by either test, so never disqualify on one failed test alone. Unaided 4 ft in one ear + aided 5 ft in the other = passes (better-ear rule).
  • Audiometry: averages R 42.3 / L 44.0 → do not certify (may qualify if a repeat with an aid passes); 30+48+60 (avg 46) fails; R 32+40+42 (avg 38) passes → certify.
  • Frequencies 500/1,000/2,000 Hz; 3,000 Hz is not part of the standard; calibration is to ANSI Z24.5-1951 (ISO results get converted).
  • Hearing aid drivers: cert 2 years; whisper with the aid is acceptable; an audiologist is optional when office testing suffices; a cochlear implant needs no exemption.
  • Exemption: FMCSA decides; motor carrier ensures documentation; MER "Accompanied by a waiver/exemption" + "hearing"; max 2 years.
