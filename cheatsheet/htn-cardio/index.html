Hypertension & Cardiovascular

Hypertension — 49 CFR 391.41(b)(6)

Standard: no current clinical diagnosis of high BP likely to interfere with safe CMV operation. HTN alone rarely causes sudden collapse; risk rises with target-organ damage, esp. cerebrovascular disease. Advisory criteria = recommendations, not mandatory (2002 CV Advisory Panel; adopted JNC-6, 1997). 39% of drivers with >20 yrs experience have HTN; drivers more likely than other professions [training].

FMCSA staging / certification

Stage BP Certification
I 140/90 – 159/99 Low risk. 1 yr on first instance; recert annually, BP should be ≤140/90. At a subsequent exam, if >140/90 but <160/100 → one-time 3-month cert to reach ≤140/90
II 160/100 – 179/109 Absolute indication for drug therapy. One-time 3-month cert to start/adjust meds → then 1 yr if ≤140/90 and treatment tolerated
III ≥180/110 High risk of acute event. Do not certify, even briefly, until ≤140/90 and tolerated. Then 6 months, and every 6 months thereafter
  • Treated HTN: max 1 yr if ≤140/90 and meds tolerated (140/90 exactly = controlled → 1 yr). Never 2 yrs on antihypertensives.
  • Annual cert when ME does not know pre-treatment severity.
  • Secondary HTN: same stages. Evaluate for secondary cause if persistently hypertensive on max/near-max doses of 2–3 agents; some causes fixable by surgery/specific drugs.
  • Complications (ESRD, organ damage, PAD) → evaluate under the applicable other standard (PAD → (b)(4)).
  • ME may always DQ when condition/prognosis uncertain (166/102 on meds + new headaches/dizziness → DQ pending treating-provider eval).

BP measurement / exam

  • Use readings taken during the exam. Confirm any reading >139/89 with a second measurement later in the exam (systolic or diastolic alone counts). The 2024 MEH states that readings greater than 139/89 should be confirmed with a second measurement later in the exam — guidance ('should'), not regulation. Fluctuation causes: circadian cycle, emotional/physical state, white-coat, left vs right arm, technique — any of these can move a reading.
  • Trained assistive personnel may measure/record BP; ME personally confirms any disqualifying reading [training].
  • Must measure BP and pulse (rate, strength, rhythm); record in MCSA-5875 Testing section, extra readings in "Second Reading" or abnormal-answer box. Document all BP readings taken.
  • Advise: failure to reach ≤140/90 disqualifies; encourage PCP care.

Hypertensive emergency

Meningismus, sudden neuro deficits, abrupt SOB, tearing/ripping chest or back pain → stop driving, immediate emergency care (184/118 + SOB + dizziness = emergency, not just DQ). A driver at imminent risk of an acute hypertensive event is not certifiable until that risk is controlled.

Antihypertensives — side effects

  • General: sleepiness, light-headedness, syncope, headache; evaluate individually; warn driver.
  • Lisinopril: dizziness, headache, blurred vision. Enalapril + furosemide → ask about HTN vs CHF history (choose the right standard).
  • Verapamil/CCBs: bradycardia (pulse 52 + fatigue → assess side effects; brady → dizziness, syncope, sudden death).
  • Bisoprolol: most concerning = dizziness/weakness. Atorvastatin: drowsiness. Clopidogrel: bleeding.
  • Sodium channel blockers: qualify under the underlying condition's standard — arrhythmia (b)(4), epilepsy (b)(8).
  • Request provider info via CMV Driver Medication form MCSA-5895.

Cardiovascular — 49 CFR 391.41(b)(4)

Note: the waiting periods, "1 yr" intervals and ETT/LVEF cut-offs in the tables below are the older Appendix A / 2002 Cardiovascular Advisory Panel guidance still taught by most training courses. The 2024 ME Handbook lists these conditions as "up to 2 years" with individual assessment. Know both; see the final section.

Standard: no current clinical diagnosis of MI, angina pectoris, coronary insufficiency, thrombosis, or other CV disease known to be accompanied by syncope, dyspnea, collapse, or congestive cardiac failure.

Advisory criteria

  • "Current clinical diagnosis" = current or not fully stabilized. "Known to be accompanied by" = accompanied by or likely to cause the 4 symptoms.
  • CABG and pacemaker = remedial → do not preclude certification. ICD treats an ongoing condition; syncope/collapse from disease and discharge → not certifiable.
  • Anticoagulation alone never disqualifies; judge the underlying condition and general health.

Exam principles

  • Fitness includes capacity for strenuous labor; job tasks are deciding factors. Severe cerebrovascular disease → high MI/sudden-death rate.
  • Cardiologist recommendation is needed but not the decision. ME applies the (b)(4) criteria and must weigh actual test results, not clearance letters alone; historical tables require "clearance from a CV specialist who understands the job of commercial driving." Obtain records; attach tests to MCSA-5875.
  • Undiagnosed findings (irregular rhythm at 132 bpm, S4/atrial gallop, exertional leg pain + fatigue, pulsatile abdominal mass) → DQ/refer before certifying, no interim cert.

Cardiac tests

  • Ordered by ME, PCP, cardiologist, or CV surgeon; interpreted by a cardiologist; record in MER Section 6 or attach. ME may order ETT, echo, ECG — any.
  • ETT: most common; workload in METs; ≥6 METs = heavy work of commercial driving. Satisfactory: >6 METs through Bruce stage 2, HR ≥85% predicted max (unless beta-blocked), SBP rise ≥20 mmHg without angina, no significant ST depression.
  • Echo/LVEF: 55–70% normal; 40–54% slightly low; 35–39% moderately low; <35% severely low. Imaging beats plain ETT; use when resting ECG abnormal or ETT nondiagnostic.
  • Post-MI ETT at 4–6 weeks; repeat at least every 2 years (source ties biennial ETT to CHD/post-MI follow-up; no age-45 rule in these materials).

Coronary heart disease

LV function = major independent predictor of survival; also general health, age, arrhythmias, angina, vascular disease, severity. Ask: tx effective/safe/stable? knows meds? compliant?

Condition Wait Certify if Max Do not certify if
Acute MI min 2 months asymptomatic; tolerates meds; satisfactory ETT; resting LVEF ≥40% (in-hospital post-MI echo ≥40% suffices initially); no ischemic ECG 1 yr rest angina/pattern change within 3 months; ischemic rest ECG; intolerant of therapy
Angina ≥3 months free of rest angina/pattern change stable, asymptomatic, tolerates meds, satisfactory ETT 1 yr unstable angina within 3 months
PCI 1 week after elective uncomplicated PCI for stable angina per FMCSA's 2002 Cardiovascular Advisory Panel (longer post-MI/unstable); many training courses teach 3 weeks — be ready for either asymptomatic; tolerates meds; no access-site injury (heals ~1 wk); cardiologist clearance 1 yr access-site complications; rest angina; ischemic ECG
CABG ≥3 months any type asymptomatic; no orthostatic sx; LVEF ≥40%; healed sternum; cardiologist approval 1 yr symptomatic; orthostatic sx; LVEF <40%; unhealed sternum; no cardiologist eval
Heart failure none (etiology confirmed, tx stable) asymptomatic; tolerates meds; ETT >6 METs; LVEF ≥40%, stable; compliant 1 yr symptomatic HF regardless of EF; asymptomatic LVEF <40%; asymptomatic LVEF ≤50% + ventricular arrhythmias (VT/NSVT/palpitations)
  • Stable angina triggers: exertion, emotion, weather extremes, sex. Unstable: rest pain, ↑frequency/duration, ↓med response → precursor to syncope/collapse/CHF.
  • Post-MI: highest mortality first months (mostly sudden death); return to work if no exercise ischemia or LV dysfunction.
  • CABG: for multivessel CHD, proximal left main, extensive disease with LV dysfunction/debilitating angina. Sternal risk first 3 months; graft re-occlusion typically after 5 yrs; ETT may be indicated (not required); most drivers return to work.

Arrhythmias & devices

Ask: underlying disease disqualifying? sudden-death risk? cerebral hypoperfusion/LOC risk? Most sudden cardiac deaths = VT/VF, usually no prior dx; recurrence high even after defibrillation.

Condition Wait Certify if Max Do not certify if
ICD / ICD-pacemaker combo — Never (even asymptomatic with cardiologist clearance). Disabled ICD: only if underlying condition resolved, case-by-case — any ICD
Pacemaker (bradycardia: sinus node dysfunction, AV block) remedial; syncope table: 3 months post-implant no malfunction (syncope, persistent slow HR, fatigue, brady–tachy alternation); tx stable 1 yr malfunction; disqualifying underlying disease
SVT / AF 1 month (AF anticoagulated for stroke/risk/post-thoracic surgery; post-isthmus ablation; asymptomatic AVNRT, AVRT/WPW, atrial/junctional tach) HR controlled; anti-embolic tx effective/tolerated; no underlying heart disease; CV specialist clearance 1 yr LOC, compromised cerebral function, sudden-death resuscitation
Ventricular arrhythmia 1 month after therapy (CHD, RVOT VT, idiopathic LV VT) asymptomatic; identified non-disqualifying cause; specialist clearance 1 yr symptomatic; sustained VT ≥30 s needing intervention; NSVT or LVEF <0.40; HCM; cardiac arrest; non-spontaneous sustained VT; abnormal exercise BP; family hx premature death; syncope
Autonomic neuropathy / POTS none etiology confirmed; cardiologist-confirmed stable tx 2 yrs resting tachycardia or orthostatic BP; other-organ neuropathy impairing driving
  • AF: risk = embolus → stroke/syncope/collapse; anticoagulation lowers embolization. Decision hinges on whether underlying heart disease is disqualifying. New AF on warfarin, no INR results → DQ pending INR.

Syncope

Symptom, not diagnosis. Cardiac syncope → CV standard; neuro causes (migraine, seizure) → their own standards. Ask: pre-syncope vs true LOC? meds causing electrolyte shifts? cause? treated? tx stable?

Type Wait Certify if Max
Cardiac-based (incl. vasovagal) none asymptomatic; etiology identified and ≥1 month on treatment, or ≥3 months post-pacemaker 1 yr
Neurocardiogenic 3 months after treatment symptom relief, asymptomatic (pacemaker fixes only the cardioinhibitory, not vasodepressor, component) 1 yr; DQ if symptomatic

Valvular disease

  • Murmur grades I–VI (III = easily heard; IV = + thrill; V = partial stethoscope contact; VI = off skin). Pathologic → evaluate: holosystolic, harsh, abnormal heart sound, early/mid-systolic click, grade ≥III, left sternal border.
  • Mechanical valves: no rejection, durable, need anticoagulation. Biologic: may be rejected, last 7–10 yrs, usually no long-term anticoagulation.
Condition Wait Certify if Max Do not certify if
AR mild–moderate none asymptomatic; moderate needs normal LV function, little/no enlargement 1 yr symptoms; moderate + abnormal LV; >mild LV enlargement
AR severe 3 months after AV repair no sx; normal LV; LVEDD ≤60 / LVESD ≤50 mm 6 months unrepaired; 1 yr repaired symptoms; <6 METs; EF <50%; LVEDD >70 / LVESD >55 mm
Aortic stenosis 3 months if repaired mild or moderate asymptomatic; severe only if repaired 1 yr moderate + angina/HF/AF/EF <50%/thromboembolism; severe AS regardless of sx/LV function
AV repair / prosthetic valve 3 months LVEF >40%; anticoag compliant; specialist clearance 1 yr persistent sx; EF <40%; thromboembolism; AF; pulmonary HTN; inadequate INR (checks ≥monthly)
Mitral regurgitation 3 months post-repair/commissurotomy asymptomatic mild–moderate with normal LV & PA pressure; asymptomatic severe; or repaired + clearance 1 yr symptoms; <6 METs; ruptured chordae/flail leaflet; AF; LV dysfunction; thromboembolism; pulmonary HTN
Mitral stenosis 4 wks post-balloon valvotomy; 3 months post-surgical commissurotomy mild/moderate asymptomatic; severe only after treatment + clearance 1 yr severe MS until treated; thromboembolism; pulmonary HTN
MVP none ME judges not dangerous 1 yr symptoms/reduced effort tolerance; flail/ruptured chordae; emboli; AF; syncope or VT; severe MR/LV dysfunction
Pulmonary stenosis 1 month post-balloon; 3 months post-surgical mild/moderate or corrected 1 yr dyspnea/palpitations/syncope; peak gradient >50 mmHg; RV pressure >50% systemic; >mild RVH/RV dysfunction; >moderate PR; main PA >5 cm
  • Mitral stenosis: most important = symptom development (angina, syncope, fatigue, dyspnea). MR (most common valve disease): dyspnea, fatigue, orthopnea, PND = poor prognosis. AR: severity + LV size/function + symptoms.

Aneurysm, PVD, venous disease

Condition Wait Certify if Max Do not certify if
Aneurysm post-repair 3 months asymptomatic; follow-up compliant; specialist clearance; anticoag monitoring 1 yr symptomatic; repair recommended but not done
Acute DVT none (etiology confirmed, tx stable) no residual acute DVT; anticoag monitoring 1 yr ineffectively treated
Chronic thrombotic venous disease none asymptomatic 2 yrs ME judges dangerous
Claudication / PVD 3 months post-surgical repair asymptomatic; no other disqualifying CV condition 1 yr pain at rest
Pulmonary embolism 3 months PE-free appropriate long-term treatment; anticoag monitoring 1 yr symptoms
Superficial phlebitis none otherwise qualified 1 yr coexisting DVT not meeting DVT rules
Varicose veins none no complications 2 yrs venous insufficiency/ulcers/recurrent DVT
  • AAA: rupture risk tied to size (ME's primary concern). Men 3:1, 60s–70s; smoking strongest risk factor; family hx, Caucasian. Exam detects ~90% of aneurysms >6 cm; bruit; ultrasound ~100% sensitive/specific for monitoring. Thoracic asymptomatic <5 cm unlikely to rupture. Pulsatile mass → DQ pending CV consult, CT/US, specialist recommendations. (No 4–5 cm tiers in these materials.)
  • DVT: drivers at higher risk (sitting); anticoagulation cuts recurrence ~80%. Fresh DVT on warfarin without records → DQ pending treating-physician consult. Remote DVT clearance factors: symptoms, proximal extension, muscular-vein thrombus, D-dimer, ultrasound, bleeding risk.
  • Claudication (7–9% of PVD): decide on etiology confirmed + tx effective/safe/stable, not the surgeon's note.

Anticoagulation

Historical: wait 1 month after stabilization; max 1 yr; INR results at exam, INR monitoring ≥monthly; DQ if INR unmonitored/non-therapeutic or underlying disease disqualifying. Decision = underlying disorder, not the drug. (DOACs not addressed in these materials.)

Other conditions

Condition Guidance
Heart transplant Concerns: rejection, post-transplant atherosclerosis, med effects. Historical: wait 6 months, cert 6 months, NYHA I–II, LVEF >40%, no rejection, specialist clearance. Practical rule: evaluate individually
HCM Case-by-case; consider cardiology. Historical certify: no arrest, no VT/NSVT, no family hx premature sudden death, no syncope, septum <30 mm, clearance; DQ if peak gradient ≥50. Sudden death may be first sign
Restrictive CM Least common; stiff myocardium, impaired filling; arrhythmias common. Case-by-case
Congenital HD HF and sudden death = major killers. Ask anatomy, severity, repair, cardiomegaly, sx, adult-CHD cardiologist. Ebstein: 1 yr if mild/asymptomatic; DQ if moderate–severe, intracardiac lesion, shunt, symptomatic arrhythmia, accessory pathway
Brugada/long QT, myocarditis/pericarditis, hypotension Not addressed in these materials

High-yield points

  • ≥180/110 → do not certify, even briefly (184/112 — or 178/112, disqualifying on the diastolic alone → no certificate; advise prompt care). Once ≤140/90 → 6 months, then every 6 months.
  • First encounter at Stage 2 (168/94, or 162/88 on the systolic alone) → one-time 3-month cert; the ME certifies for 3 months rather than disqualifying, and prescribing is the treating provider's job.
  • Stage 1 untreated (150/94, 144/92) → 1 yr; Stage 1 = low risk.
  • On meds, 140/90 × 2 → 1 yr; treated HTN never 2 yrs.
  • Returning driver >140/90 but <160/100 (e.g., 156/84 — the systolic alone counts) → one-time 3-month cert.
  • Confirm any reading >139/89 with a second measurement; assistants may measure, ME confirms disqualifying values [training]; document all readings.
  • 184/118 + SOB/dizziness → immediate emergency care.
  • Medication side effects worth knowing: antihypertensives as a class → syncope; verapamil → bradycardia; bisoprolol → dizziness/weakness; atorvastatin → drowsiness [course — clinically dubious]; clopidogrel → bleeding; request prescriber input via MCSA-5895.
  • ICD or ICD-pacemaker combo → not certifiable, even with cardiologist clearance. CABG, pacemaker = remedial.
  • Anticoagulation decision = underlying disease. AF on warfarin → INR results first. Asymptomatic AF → is underlying disease disqualifying?
  • Newly found irregular rhythm at 132 bpm → disqualify and refer; no interim certificate.
  • Post-MI 3 months, satisfactory ETT, EF 48%, cleared → 1 yr. MI 5 wks, EF 52%, no ETT yet → request the ETT (4–6 wks post-MI; repeat q2 yrs).
  • Stable angina 1 yr with ETT + clearance → 1 yr; inconclusive ETT + recent exertional pain → DQ pending testing.
  • PCI 3 wks → cardiologist clearance, check access site, compliance.
  • CHD with NSVT, borderline METs, EF 36% → weigh LVEF stability, med compliance, and symptoms together.
  • HCM, restrictive CM, transplant, pulmonary stenosis → case-by-case / nature & severity.
  • Mitral stenosis → watch symptom development; AR → weigh severity, LV size/function, and symptoms together.
  • S4 gallop → cardiology referral.
  • AAA → size; pulsatile mass → DQ pending consult/imaging.
  • Sodium channel blocker → most applicable standard.
  • 6 METs; tests interpreted by cardiologist; ME relies on results, not clearance letters.
