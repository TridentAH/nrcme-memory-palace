Seizures, Loss of Consciousness & Diabetes

PART A – EPILEPSY, SEIZURES, LOSS OF CONSCIOUSNESS

Standard – 49 CFR 391.41(b)(8)

  • Qualified if no established history or clinical diagnosis of epilepsy or any other condition likely to cause loss of consciousness (LOC) or loss of ability to control a CMV.
  • Condition not presenting with seizures/LOC (or low likelihood) → evaluate under a different standard.
  • Review/discuss every "yes" on MCSA-5875. If seizures reported: ask about childhood febrile, provoked, and unprovoked seizures and how many.

Definitions

Term Key points
Epilepsy Chronic functional disease; seizures occur without warning, loss of voluntary control, may cause LOC.
Single unprovoked seizure No known cause/trigger. Not epilepsy. Recurrence most likely within 5 yrs, very low after. Risk factors: remote neuro insult (stroke), abnormal EEG, focal lesion on imaging, family history.
Second unprovoked seizure Regardless of interval → may = history/diagnosis of epilepsy.
Provoked seizure Known cause or clear reversible/avoidable trigger: drug reaction, high temperature, acute infection, dehydration, alcohol/drug withdrawal, acute metabolic disturbance (hypernatremia, hypoglycemia, hypomagnesemia). Normal reaction of healthy nervous system; recurrence risk = likelihood trigger recurs.
Seizure after stroke Even a single one may = epilepsy.
Childhood febrile seizure Age 6 mo–5 yr, healthy child, fever from infection; no adult residuals. Confirm history limited to febrile seizures → certify normally (2 yr).

Cannot be certified (advisory criteria)

  1. History of epilepsy/seizure disorder (unless 10-yr criterion met or federal seizure exemption).
  2. Current clinical diagnosis of epilepsy/seizure disorder.
  3. Taking anti-seizure medication to prevent seizures.

  • Stroke/TIA/hemorrhage: need normal neurological, neuro-ophthalmological, neuropsychology evals; no residuals; neurologist clearance. No cert if on oral anticoagulant or CNS-depressant med.
  • Seizure risk by location: cerebellum/brainstem = not increased; cortical/subcortical = increased.
  • Stroke: recovery and recurrence mostly within 1 yr (recurrence 10–15%). TIA: 1 in 3 → stroke; ~half within 1 yr.

Federal Seizure Exemption

  • Shorter wait than 5/10 yrs. ME may refer when driver has epilepsy/seizure disorder, a single non-epileptic seizure, or ME is unsure driver meets (b)(8).
History Seizure-free Meds
Epilepsy / seizure disorder 8 yrs on or off meds (if stopped: 8 yrs from discontinuation) If on meds: stable 2 yrs (no change in drug, dose, frequency)
Single unprovoked seizure 4 yrs on or off meds Stable 2 yrs if on meds
Provoked, low risk: med-induced; non-penetrating head injury LOC ≤ 30 min; brief LOC; non-recurring metabolic derangement; drug/alcohol withdrawal Case-specific —
Provoked, moderate-high risk: non-penetrating head injury LOC/amnesia > 30 min; penetrating head injury; intracerebral/intracranial hemorrhage; brain surgery with significant hemorrhage; brain tumor; stroke 8 yrs on or off meds —
  • ME: full exam, other standards met; max cert 1 yr (epilepsy w/ exemption), 2 yrs (single unprovoked seizure w/ exemption); check "accompanied by" exemption on MCSA-5875, write "seizure exemption."
  • Carrier ensures current exemption + current MEC (MCSA-5876); driver carries both.
  • Apply: 202-366-4001; FMCSAseizureexemptions@dot.gov.

Anti-seizure meds not for seizures

  • Ask why prescribed; evaluate under that diagnosis (lamotrigine for bipolar → (b)(9)); document e.g., "gabapentin for nerve pain" on MCSA-5875.
  • Side effects: mood depression, cognitive defects, slowed reflexes, unsteadiness, sedation; low pain doses less likely to interfere.

  • Phenobarbital (Luminal): anticonvulsant + controlled substance → also (b)(12)(ii) prescription exception; MCSA-5895 may be used.

  • Tests: EEG (epilepsy, sleep disorders); MRI; lumbar puncture (tumor, infection, bleeding). Refer to PCP; document requests.

Syncope

  • Symptom, not a condition. Neurologic cause → (b)(8)/underlying standard; cardiac cause → cardiovascular standard.
  • Pre-syncope (dizziness) vs true syncope (LOC); check med side effects (electrolyte shifts), cause, treatment. Single unknown-cause LOC → 6-month wait.

Narcolepsy / idiopathic hypersomnia

  • Narcolepsy: excessive daytime sleepiness (primary sign) + REM phenomena awake (cataplexy, sleep paralysis, hypnagogic hallucinations); naps 10–20 min up to 8×/day; sleep attacks almost daily; no cure. Dx: overnight lab sleep study + Multiple Sleep Latency Test next morning.
  • Both conditions: does NOT meet (b)(8), even if treated.
  • Xyrem (sodium oxybate, Schedule III) → ask underlying condition; may use Pending.

Headache, vertigo, Meniere's

  • Only headaches likely to cause LOC/loss of control preclude certification. Migraine not interfering → up to 2 yrs. Consider frequency/severity/duration, symptoms (visual, light/noise sensitivity), med effects, treatment stable.
  • Vertigo: needs vestibular, visual, proprioceptive input + CNS integration. Vertigo meds (meclizine) sedate. Untreated/uncontrolled → disqualify. [legacy FMCSA tables — not in the current (Jan 2024) Appendix A]: BPV / peripheral vestibulopathy → 2 months symptom-free, max 2 yrs; the same legacy tables list Meniere's, labyrinthine fistula, nonfunctioning labyrinths as do-not-certify, but the 2024 MEH (and most courses) treat Meniere's case-by-case (frequency, warning?, LOC likely?, treatment stable?).
  • Meniere's: inner ear, usually one ear; vertigo 20 min–hours; fluctuating hearing loss, tinnitus, ear pressure; permanent hearing loss possible.

CNS infection / tumor / TBI considerations

  • Infection: confirm etiology; seizures (how many/when/meds/recurrence)? resolved? residuals? No records → request them.
  • Tumor: etiology; LOC likely? treatment stable? Some benign tumors certifiable after successful surgery; imaging no tumor; deficit absent/stable.
  • TBI: LOC duration? cognitive/psychosocial/sensory/motor severity? Post-TBI modafinil → assess alertness/attention, side effects (nervousness, anxiety, headache). Dementia → most likely precludes certification.

PART B – DIABETES

Regulations

  • 391.41(b)(3): no history/diagnosis of diabetes treated with insulin unless meets 391.46.
  • 391.46 (2018 rule): ITDM drivers no longer need a federal exemption. Qualified if otherwise meets 391.41 (or has required exemption/SPE) + evaluated by treating clinician + certification exam by ME.
  • Non-insulin diabetes: no specific standard; must meet the 13 standards; case-by-case.

ITDM process

Item Rule
Treating clinician (manages diabetes, prescribes insulin) Evaluates before every cert/recert; completes & signs MCSA-5870 with date, full name, office address, phone
Timing ME exam within 45 days; driver gives MCSA-5870 to ME; ME retains it with MER
ME Considers form, applies independent judgment; may request more info
Waiting period / max cert None / 12 months

Four 391.46 standards

  1. Stable insulin regimen and proper control.
  2. No severe non-proliferative or proliferative diabetic retinopathy — disqualifying by regulation; MCSA-5870 documents it; ME may rely on it or refer for further eval. Risk: sudden vision loss (bleed/detachment); treatment impairs peripheral & night vision.
  3. ≥ preceding 3 months of electronic glucose self-monitoring records to clinician → 12-month cert.
  4. Without 3 months of records → cannot certify > 3 months.
  • Records: electronic glucometer storing all readings with date/time, electronically downloadable; printout or meter to clinician.
  • < 3 months: ME may certify 3 months → driver collects 3 months → clinician completes new MCSA-5870 → back to ME → up to 12 months.

Certify vs do not certify (ITDM)

Certify Do NOT certify
Meets 391.46 + 391.41; clinician attests stable plan/well controlled; valid completed MCSA-5870 Unstable regimen or poor control; < 3 months records; severe hypoglycemic episode not yet cleared under 391.46(e) (common training-course convention: "episode in last 12 months" → do not certify); any other (b) disqualifier

Severe hypoglycemic episode

  • Definition: requires assistance from others, or causes LOC, seizure, or coma.
  • This is the regulation's mechanism (391.46(e)): driver prohibited from CMV operation; report and be evaluated by clinician as soon as practical. To resume: clinician confirms cause addressed + stable regimen/control, completes new MCSA-5870; driver keeps it for next ME exam. The MCSA-5870 asks about the preceding 3 months; the "12-month window" is a training-course convention, not the regulation.
  • Hypoglycemia = greatest public-safety risk among diabetic complications.
  • Non-insulin driver, severe episode 1 month ago → disqualify, advise treating-provider follow-up. Frequent hypoglycemia → evaluate under (b)(8).

Glucose / labs

  • No mandated glucose or HbA1c level; ME judgment. Reference: fasting ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L); non-fasting > 200 (11.1); A1c ≥ 6.5% diabetic, 5.7–6.4% at risk.
  • Every exam: note sugar, blood, protein in urine; ask about diabetes/kidney disease; with consent consult provider (meds, side effects, A1c, creatinine, BUN). Glycosuria → consult treating provider. Persistent proteinuria → neuropathy/renal dysfunction, consider testing.

Non-insulin-treated diabetes

  • Wait none; max 2 yrs if well controlled (App. A: meets all standards, no underlying condition, plan without insulin not interfering with driving).
  • MCSA-5872 = optional info tool (diagnosis, monitoring, stability).
  • Oral agents / GLP-1 (Ozempic): no specific guideline; check side effects (dizziness, headache, fatigue).
  • Ask: symptoms (polyuria, thirst/hunger, weight change, numb hands/feet)? glycosuria tested? hypoglycemia/LOC? treatment stable? compliant?
  • Complications under other standards: amputation/neuropathy (b)(1),(2),(7); cardiac (b)(4); retinopathy (b)(7); HTN (b)(6).

Renal / GU

  • No ESRD/dialysis standard → evaluate under underlying cause: diabetes → (b)(3); HTN/CHF → (b)(4). Ask about post-dialysis fatigue, cramps, hypotension, cognitive impairment; compliance. The MCSA-5875 physical exam includes the genito-urinary system including hernias (item 9).

High-yield points

  • Single unprovoked seizure → 5 yrs seizure-free off meds. Epilepsy → 10 yrs seizure-free off meds; 2 years seizure-free is never enough without an exemption.
  • 3 unprovoked seizures in 5 yrs → disqualify outright; deferral or referral adds nothing.
  • Exemption: epilepsy 8 yrs + meds stable 2 yrs; single unprovoked 4 yrs. Max cert with exemption: epilepsy 1 yr, single unprovoked 2 yrs.
  • ICU hypotension/sepsis seizure, resolved, no meds → certify 2 yrs (no wait). Non-seizure LOC → 6 months. Febrile seizure at age 4 → certify 2 yrs.
  • A single seizure after a stroke may by itself constitute epilepsy; provoked triggers (dehydration, childhood fever) carry no such weight.
  • Cerebellar stroke, phenytoin stopped, no seizures → assess against standard. Cerebellar AVM bleed → assess individually. Ruptured AVM + hemiplegia + cognitive deficit → disqualify.
  • TIA: 1 in 3 eventually have a stroke, about half within a year; after the 1-yr wait, max cert is 1 yr.
  • Meningitis, no records → request hospital records. Meningitis with early seizures → weigh frequency, etiology, and medications together.
  • Anti-seizure meds may sedate; lamotrigine prescribed for bipolar → evaluate under (b)(9); phenobarbital → the first question is why it was prescribed.
  • Xyrem → underlying condition; narcolepsy fails (b)(8).
  • Meclizine/BPV → assess side effects; BPV → ask frequency/severity/duration; vertigo meds → drowsiness; uncontrolled vertigo → disqualify; the symptom that matters most for driving is the dizziness itself (training courses key "dizziness").
  • Meniere's, myotonia, Bell's palsy → case-by-case. Migraine → up to 2 yrs. Benign spinal tumor → certifiable after successful surgery. Dementia → most likely precludes. Post-TBI modafinil → assess alertness/attention. TBI → memory, speech, and emotional changes all matter.
  • ITDM: 391.46 sets no A1c ceiling and no defined "safe glucose range"; carrying rapid-acting glucose is recommended, not required [training]; the diabetes exemption program no longer exists; max 1 yr; new to insulin with <3 months of records → certify up to 3 months only if the clinician attests a stable regimen (training courses key "disqualify" for a driver with no records at all); 1 month of records + good control → 3 months; Levemir = insulin → 391.46; unstable regimen disqualifies (training-course convention: an episode 18 months ago and neuropathy do not — the regulation's actual bar is 391.46(e), prohibited until cleared + new MCSA-5870).
  • Severe hypoglycemia is defined by events — needs assistance / LOC / seizure / coma — not by a glucose number; action = prohibited from driving, evaluate ASAP.
  • Non-insulin: max 2 yrs (diet-controlled, A1c 7.4 → 2 yrs); Ozempic → no specific guidelines; symptomatic on glimepiride → MCSA-5872; glycosuria → consult provider; proliferative retinopathy = disqualifying by regulation; dialysis → underlying condition; most common diabetic eye disease = retinopathy.
