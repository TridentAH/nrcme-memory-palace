Forms, Recordkeeping & Exam Prep

49 CFR 391.43 – Who performs the exam / rules

Rule Detail
Who performs certification exams ME listed on the National Registry of Certified Medical Examiners
Exception 1 Licensed ophthalmologist/optometrist may do the visual acuity, field of vision, color recognition portion only
Exception 2 Certified VA ME may examine veteran drivers only
ME must be knowledgeable about Physical/mental demands of commercial driving; FMCSA qualification requirements; medical advisory criteria; proficient in/uses required medical protocols
Exempt intracity zone driver Must give ME a copy of the initial medical findings that enabled intracity-zone certification
Final authority on qualification The medical examiner (not FMCSA, PCP, or employer)

FMCSA Forms – Quick Reference

Form Number Purpose Completed by Retention / handling
Medical Examination Report (MER) MCSA-5875 Records the full exam + determination Section 1 driver; Section 2 ME ME keeps ≥3 yrs from exam date; copy to driver on request; to employer only with driver's consent; to FMCSA within 48 hrs of request; contains private health info (HIPAA)
Medical Examiner's Certificate (MEC) MCSA-5876 Proof of qualification ME; signed by both ME and driver Original to driver (paper to non-CDL drivers); copy to employer on request; ME keeps ≥3 yrs from issue date; to FMCSA within 48 hrs; no private health info
CMV Driver Medical Examination Results MCSA-5850 Electronic transmission of exam results to National Registry (also used to update a pending exam) ME via National Registry account Submit by midnight local time, next calendar day after exam
Insulin-Treated Diabetes Mellitus Assessment MCSA-5870 Required to certify an insulin-treated driver (391.46); attests stable insulin regimen + properly controlled DM Driver's treating clinician To ME within 45 days of completion; file with MER
Vision Evaluation Report MCSA-5871 Required for Alternative Vision Standard (driver fails acuity/field standard, e.g., monocular) Ophthalmologist or optometrist To ME within 45 days of completion; file with MER + MEC
Non-Insulin-Treated DM Assessment MCSA-5872 Optional; info on diagnosis, monitoring, stability of non-insulin DM — (not specified) File with MER
CMV Driver Medication Form MCSA-5895 Optional; asks why meds prescribed and whether med/underlying condition could impair driving Treating physician on ME request File with MER

MER (MCSA-5875) – Section 1: Driver

Item Detail
Medical record number (top right) ME's field; [training] may be left blank
Personal info Name as on license, address, phone, age, DOB, license number + state; has CLP/CDL or applying
ID verification ME verifies identity, notes photo ID type (CDL, passport)
Prior certification Ever denied or issued for <2 years?
Health history Surgery (date/type); all meds incl. Rx, OTC, supplements; 32 health conditions (now/ever); space for unlisted conditions; "yes" answers must be explained (e.g., type of heart disease)
Signature Driver signs/dates → certifies the information is accurate and complete (the form's words)

MER – Section 2: Medical Examiner

Driver Health History Review

  • Discuss every "yes" or "not sure" response; investigate unclear answers (diagnosis, history, treatment, causes, response).
  • Compare medication list vs. history; review all meds incl. OTC – side effects, interactions (drugs, alcohol, foods, supplements), missed-dose effects.
  • Document anything the driver omitted; this space is for the details of driver-reported conditions.
  • Best practice: records from treating provider (with driver consent); order tests/consults as needed.

Testing (page 2) – four required tests

Test Recording rules
Blood pressure / pulse Record pulse rate, height, weight, BP; if a second reading is needed, record it too
Urinalysis Specific gravity, protein, blood, sugar; numerical values when indicated, or "trace/negative/none"; follow dipstick box instructions. Normal [clinical]: SG 1.005–1.030 (avg 1.020); protein/blood/sugar negative
Vision Snellen-comparable acuity; distinguish red, green, amber; note monocular vision, referral to ophthalmologist/optometrist, whether documentation received
Hearing Forced whisper or audiometry
  • [training] Trained assistants may perform vision, hearing, BP, urine tests – but the ME is responsible for and attests to all results.
  • Optional tests (spirometry, grip strength, SpO2) go in "Other testing if indicated" (page 3); copies may be attached. ECG and drug tests are not required tests.

Physical Examination

  • Check each body system normal/abnormal; discuss abnormalities in the space provided and whether they affect safe CMV operation.
  • Memorize body-system numbering on the form: 1 = general, 2 = skin, 3 = eyes, and so forth.
  • Eyes: the MER's eye row of the physical exam centers on pupillary equality and reactivity.

ME Determination (federal section = exams per 391.41–391.49; must be filled out completely)

Option How documented
Does not meet standards Check box on MER (not MEC); write explanation; discuss reason, safety impact, steps for reconsideration
Meets standards; qualified for 2-year certification Issue 2-yr MEC
Meets standards, periodic monitoring required Explain why; check period: 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, or Other (specify)
Certification categories Check all that apply: corrective lenses, hearing aid, SPE certificate, waiver/exemption
Determination pending Need more info; no MEC issued; up to 45 days to obtain info and decide; driver return date on or before 45-day expiration; if not updated in National Registry by then, exam invalid → re-examine; update via MCSA-5850; do not use if condition interferes with safe driving
Incomplete exam Unable to complete for any reason (e.g., driver declines to finish)
ME sign-off Signature, name, address, phone, date; license # + state; occupation; National Registry number + ME certificate expiration date ([training] look it up in your NR profile – no longer printed on the certificate)

[training] Second opinion after disqualification: needs employer permission; employer may require an employer-preferred ME; second ME must get all the same health history and know it is a second opinion.

Medical Examiner's Certificate (MCSA-5876)

  • Completed when driver is physically qualified; both ME and driver sign.
  • Original to driver – drivers must carry a valid MEC when operating a CMV.
  • Copy to current/prospective employer if requested; motor carriers must maintain a copy.
  • Date on certificate = date of exam; expires midnight on expiration date; no grace period.
  • NRII rule (effective June 23, 2025): MEC for CDL/CLP drivers issued electronically – submitted to National Registry, transferred to SDLA; paper original still issued to non-CDL drivers (and on driver request).
  • As of May 2026, AK, CA, KY, LA, NH not fully NRII-implemented → paper certificates there; FMCSA currently recommends paper to all CDL/CLP drivers. Temporary exemption: paper MEC accepted as proof for 60 days after issuance, valid through October 11, 2026.

Recordkeeping & Reporting

Requirement Rule
Retain MER Every driver examined – ≥3 years from exam date
Retain MEC Every driver certified – ≥3 years from issue date
Records requests Available to FMCSA / federal, state, local enforcement within 48 hours
Transmit results MCSA-5850 by midnight local time, next calendar day after the exam; [training] submitted through the NR account's driver-exam screens; include state-specific info for state exams
No exams in a month Report via NR account by the last day of the month

ME certification maintenance (FMCSA audits)

  • Update NR profile within 30 days of change; [training] periodic review (e.g., quarterly) recommended. 5-yr refresher in year 5; 10-yr recertification training + exam in year 9 or 10.
  • Provide all exam records (MER, MEC, 5870, 5871, 5895, 5872, other records used) within 48 hrs; correct CDL/CLP applicant errors.
  • Noncompliance → Notice of Proposed Removal: acknowledge within 30 days, complete corrective actions within 60 days, or be removed; wait 30 days to request reinstatement (not guaranteed).

NRCME Certification Exam – Format & Logistics

Item Detail
Format Closed-book, computer-facilitated, 2 continuous hours, 120 questions, 4 options each — select the single best answer
Scored [training] 100 scored; 20 unscored pilot items (indistinguishable)
Passing 71%
Versions [training] Multiple test forms exist; difficulty may vary
Eligibility Meet 49 CFR 390.103 (licensed/registered/certified by state to do physicals: MD, DO, DC, APN, PA, other authorized); complete an FMCSA-accredited training program; NR account + NR number
Scheduling Approved testing organization listed on FMCSA site; need NR number + training certificate; fees/locations/accommodations from testing org; remote secure-computer option may exist
Check-in [training] Testing-org procedure — verify with yours. Typically: arrive 30 min early; bring NR number, proof of medical credential, proof of training, one government-issued photo ID; no phones/notes/aids; misconduct → dismissal, score not reported
Results [training] Score same day at testing center; after FMCSA verifies credentials → email with ME certificate + NR number; do not perform DOT exams until the NR credential is received
Retake No mandated waiting period and no limit on attempts; rescheduling waits on FMCSA updating your account (can take up to 7 days); any testing org; pay the fee again

Question Levels ([training] — roughly one-third each)

Level Tests Original example (ours)
Recall Facts: standards, advisory criteria, FMCSA mission, driver population, CDL definition Which frequencies are averaged for the hearing standard? → 500, 1,000, 2,000 Hz
Application Reach conclusions from medical info; "what should ME do next"; testing, recordkeeping Driver's BP measures 176/108 at a first exam → one-time 3-month certificate (Stage 2)
Analysis Clinical scenarios + FMCSA guidance + clinical judgment; refer/disqualify/other Driver on a 2 a.m.–2 p.m. schedule, BMI 41, witnessed apneas, drowsy-driving crash last year → do not certify today; refer for sleep evaluation

[training] Two-thirds = application + analysis; expect FMCSA items beyond the curriculum (meds/side effects, normal lab values, exam skills).

Exam Content – Two Domains (FMCSA National Registry core curriculum; training required by 49 CFR 390.105)

I. Driver's Medical Information – A. Identification & history (verify ID; driver signs history; probe "yes" answers, illness/injury/surgery in last 5 years [legacy]; meds/supplements, substances). B. Physical exam (driver gowned [training]; height/weight; eyes (b)(10); ears (b)(11); mouth/throat (b)(5); neck (b)(7); heart (b)(4),(b)(6); lungs (b)(5); abdomen; spine (b)(7); extremities (b)(1),(b)(4),(b)(7); neuro (b)(7)–(b)(9); urine; mental status (b)(9)). C. Diagnostic tests/referrals (ME may order any needed test; refer to specialist, SPE/on-road eval, incidental findings). D. Ancillary documentation (5871, 5870, SPE cert, intracity exemption, SAP results). II. Determination & Disposition – A. Health education counseling (contacts → carry glasses; hearing aid → keep a spare power source; DVT risk; [legacy] the defunct Federal diabetes-exemption program — replaced by 391.46 in 2018 — required glucose checks 1 h before driving and every 4 h while driving, carrying rapid glucose, annual log submission: historical curriculum content, not current requirements). B. Risk assessment (job tasks, SPE cases, cognition, wellness stressors, sudden incapacitation). C. Certification outcome & intervals (disqualify: epilepsy/seizure history, ITDM without exemption, vision/hearing below standard without exemption, methadone (older training-material wording — current guidance says methadone does not automatically preclude; consult prescriber), current alcoholism, controlled substance without Rx; document reasons; certify for appropriate interval).

Study Tips

  • Master FMCSA regs, advisory criteria, ME Handbook 2024; know hearing/seizure exemptions, SPE conditions, disqualifying meds; body-system numbers on MER. Drill with practice questions until the patterns are automatic.
  • Think like an ME (public safety first), not a PCP. Watch absolute qualifiers ("always"/"never"); eliminate clearly wrong options before choosing the single best answer; skip hard items and return to them; answer every one of the 120; budget the full two hours; change an answer only when you have a concrete reason.

High-yield points

  • MER retention = 3 years. Records to FMCSA = 48 hours.
  • MCSA-5850 deadline = midnight local time, next calendar day after the exam.
  • MEC expires midnight on listed date, no grace period; employer must keep copy; MEC contains no private health info; post-NRII, paper original goes to non-CDL drivers.
  • The MER reaches an employer only with driver consent; it must go to FMCSA on request; it contains HIPAA-protected info.
  • Driver signature certifies the information is accurate and complete.
  • Monocular/alt vision → MCSA-5871, by ophthalmologist/optometrist, within 45 days. ITDM → MCSA-5870, treating clinician, within 45 days, used to certify insulin-treated driver.
  • MCSA-5872 = optional extra info on non-insulin DM. MCSA-5895 = optional, gathers why meds were prescribed and whether they could impair driving.
  • Four required tests: vision, hearing, urinalysis, BP/pulse. ECG and drug testing are not part of the DOT physical.
  • Spirometry/other optional results → "Other testing if indicated" (page 3).
  • "Determination pending" = ME needs test results/info obtainable within 45 days; never for a condition that currently interferes with safe driving or for an open-ended recovery.
  • Disqualification = check "Does not meet standards" on the MER; a MEC is issued only when the driver qualifies.
  • Health History Review = details of driver-reported conditions; assess meds for side effects, interactions, and missed-dose effects.
  • Physical Exam discussion space = abnormalities and effect on safe CMV operation.
  • Incidental condition outside the certification decision → discuss with the driver and refer to an appropriate provider.
  • Exempt intracity zone driver brings initial medical findings.
  • Who decides qualification: the ME. Who performs exam: NR-listed ME (exceptions: eye doctor for vision portion; VA ME for veterans). ME must know the physical and mental demands of commercial driving.
